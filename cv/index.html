Scientific AI for Geotechnical & Geohazard Systems

My research focuses on scientific AI for complex geotechnical and geohazard systems: learning system states from sparse, heterogeneous and multiscale observations, integrating physical principles and domain knowledge to understand evolution, and investigating failure forecasting, uncertainty analysis and trustworthy engineering decisions.

Research Experience

2020 – 2024

Multiscale Deformation, Failure and Landslide Instability in Fissured Expansive Soils

Investigated how fissures affect the strength, deformation and landslide instability of expansive soils. Combined fracture-mechanics experiments, DIC/resistivity monitoring, microstructural characterization and slope model testing to explain fissure propagation and instability across scales.

2024 – 2025

Tunnel Surface-Defect Recognition from Engineering Monitoring Data

Worked on deep-learning recognition, generative image completion and time-series forecasting using tunnel surface-defect and large-structure monitoring data, exploring state extraction, anomaly recognition and evolution forecasting.

2025 – present

Spatiotemporal Slope Deformation Learning and Long-term Forecasting from Sparse Observations

With STAC-GNSS as a core project, study how sparse GNSS networks can support learning of continuous slope deformation fields and evolution over multiple timescales, with further work on multisource fusion, spatial generalization and long-term forecasting.

2026 – present

Physics- and Knowledge-guided Scientific AI for Complex Geosystems

Explore the integration of data, physics and knowledge for geosystem evolution and failure, extending data-driven learning toward physical constraints, multifidelity methods, uncertainty quantification and knowledge-augmented reasoning.
