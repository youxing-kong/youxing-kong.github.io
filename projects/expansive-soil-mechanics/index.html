Multiscale geotechnical mechanics

Multiscale deformation and failure mechanisms of expansive soils

IN THIS PROJECT

Research question and methodsResultsEngineering insightsRelated papers

Research question and methods

Swelling–shrinkage deformation and slope instability in expansive soils are closely related to moisture changes, whose effects involve mineral interlayer structures, soil fracture propagation, and slope deformation. This study uses molecular dynamics simulations of montmorillonite, CCNBD fracture propagation tests, and fractured-slope model tests to investigate changes in interlayer structure, fracture-tip deformation, and slope strain evolution, respectively. The results show that hydration history affects the interlayer structure of montmorillonite, water ingress into fractures alters strain propagation and fracture resistance, and pre-existing fractures and infiltration jointly influence slope deformation and shallow sliding.

Results

Engineering insights

At the microscopic scale, path-dependent differences in water-molecule distribution and cation hydration sustain hysteresis in the interlayer structure of montmorillonite. At the specimen scale, water ingress into fractures causes localized softening and alters crack propagation. At the slope scale, pre-existing fractures affect strain transfer, loading promotes sliding along fracture interfaces, and infiltration promotes the formation of shallow slip surfaces. The model tests indicate that the middle and toe of the slope warrant particular attention in reinforcement.
