Slope monitoring & forecasting

Three-dimensional deformation prediction and monitoring optimization for open-pit mine slopes

IN THIS PROJECT

Research question and methodsResultsEngineering insightsRelated papers

Research question and methods

Monitoring open-pit mine slopes requires identifying the spatial distribution and evolution of deformation, but sparsely distributed GNSS monitoring points commissioned at different times provide observations only at limited locations. This study integrates displacement histories from target and neighboring monitoring points with terrain information to develop a spatiotemporal model for three-dimensional displacement prediction. It provides both central displacement predictions and upper prediction limits for displacement magnitude to balance prediction accuracy with the identification of areas of larger deformation. Combining the predicted deformation field with the spatial coverage of the monitoring network further yields a monitoring-point optimization scheme involving priority retention, supplementary coverage, and redundancy review.

Results

Engineering insights

Monitoring-point optimization combines regional deformation and spatial coverage. While retaining key locations and critical continuous records, it adds monitoring points in underrepresented areas and reviews proposed adjustments to redundant observations.
