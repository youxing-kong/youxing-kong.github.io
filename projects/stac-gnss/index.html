Ongoing Research

STAC-GNSS

Spatiotemporal Learning of Open-Pit Slope Deformation from Sparse GNSS Networks

Research program
Learning Geosystems from Sparse Observations ↗

Research question

How can sparse GNSS observations be used to reconstruct and forecast the continuous spatiotemporal deformation of a large slope system?

Context & data

The project investigates long-term three-dimensional GNSS displacement observations distributed across multiple zones of a large open-pit slope. The focus is the relationship between discrete monitoring points and continuous slope deformation.

Approach

  1. Sparse GNSS observations
  2. Adaptive spatial graph
  3. Spatiotemporal representation learning
  4. Deformation-field reconstruction
  5. Multi-horizon forecasting

Current findings & progress

Current focus includes adaptive graph learning, spatial relationships, continuous deformation fields, multi-horizon forecasting, spatial holdout and cross-zone generalization.

RESEARCH UPDATE · 26 AUGUST 2026

The 26 August 2026 progress report describes a TRCQ-GNSS framework: asynchronous history encoding, propagation on a fixed terrain graph and continuous spatial queries. It reports 7/30/90-day three-dimensional forecasting with strict time splits and hidden-station evaluation. This is recorded here as a dated research update; the STAC-GNSS project identity is retained. The reported application is stable to slowly deforming conditions within the same mine. Sudden acceleration and cross-mine transfer require further validation.

Scientific direction

The broader objective is not simply to predict displacement at individual sensors, but to investigate whether sparse monitoring networks contain sufficient information for AI to learn the evolving state of a continuous geosystem.

Next steps

GNSS + InSAR + terrain + environmental observations → multimodal representation → spatial generalization → physics-constrained dynamics.

Outputs

Research framework and ongoing evaluation. Public paper, code and data are forthcoming.
