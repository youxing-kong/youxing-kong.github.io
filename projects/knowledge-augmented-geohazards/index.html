Knowledge & scientific reasoning

Ontology-guided organization and engineering reuse of expansive-soil landslide knowledge

IN THIS PROJECT

Research question and methodsResultsEngineering caseEngineering insightsRelated papers

Research question and methods

Mechanism analysis and engineering treatment of expansive-soil landslides require linking dispersed knowledge of materials, hydrology, fractures, monitoring, and mitigation. Based on 536 publications, this study constructs a six-dimensional domain ontology through topic analysis and reorganization based on engineering semantics. The ontology constrains entity and relation extraction, knowledge fusion, and evidence linking. A KG-RAG framework is then developed by combining ontology routing, graph relations, and text-vector retrieval. Standardized domain question answering and the case of Landslide No. 2 at Liugang Electric Pumping Station in the Pishihang Irrigation District, Anhui, are used to examine the traceable organization and reuse of existing knowledge in new engineering contexts.

Results

Domain question answering compares the effectiveness of knowledge utilization, while the engineering case further examines the combination and reuse of knowledge under new site conditions. Together, they establish a validation chain from factual inputs to mechanism interpretation and engineering knowledge support.

Engineering case

Engineering insights

The case links irrigation and rainfall recharge at the slope crest, preferential infiltration through fractures, wetting from the canal at the slope toe, and localized softening. These connections support a mechanism interpretation involving deformation concentration in weak zones and progressive sliding, and are used to link relevant monitoring, assessment, and mitigation knowledge.
